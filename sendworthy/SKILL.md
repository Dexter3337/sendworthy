## The tone: energetic and humble

Flat emails ("I understand about your capacity.") get ignored. Emails should feel warm and forward-moving while staying modest.

- **Energy comes from specifics and momentum, not adjectives.** Thank the person for the exact thing they did, react to their news (congratulate, appreciate), and end with a concrete next step.
- **Humility comes from learning and service.** Frame the writer as someone glad to learn and to help ("I have a lot to learn from...", "I'd be glad to pitch in wherever it helps most"), not as someone selling themselves.
- **One clear line of interest is fine** ("I'm excited by the chance to...", "I'd love to..."). Do not stack enthusiasm or claim sincerity or passion.
- **Exclamation marks:** at most one per email, and only in warm exchanges (community organizers, recruiters after a helpful reply, peers). None in formal requests, submissions or admin issues.
- **Never cold, never gushing.** If a sentence would fit any email to anyone, cut it or make it specific.

## Pick the shape

- **First contact or outreach.** `Dear [known title and surname],` or `Hello [Organization] team,`. State the purpose in the first or second sentence. One sentence of relevant background, one concrete connection, one specific ask. Close `Best regards,` + signature.
- **Closing the loop after someone replies.** Always reply, even when they asked no question. Thank them for the specific help, show what you will do with it (a short list is fine for 2-4 actions), and end looking forward to the next touchpoint. 3-4 short paragraphs maximum.
- **Ongoing exchange.** Open with the answer, a specific thank-you, or the next action. `Hi [First name],` once the thread supports it. Preserve any conditions, options or commitments in short paragraphs. A simple confirmation stays one or two sentences.
- **Submission or practical request.** Say what is being sent or asked in the first sentence. Name the confirmed file or item and the action or timing needed.
- **Follow-up.** One sentence naming the earlier request, then the exact update needed and any deadline. If you caused the delay, one short apology. No restated history.
- **Declining.** Clear decision, a brief thank-you, stop.

## Length

Match the incoming email's functional brevity. Routine replies: 1-3 sentences. Loop-closing and substantive new emails: 3-4 short paragraphs, often 60-150 words. Use bullets only for distinct items the reader must check or act on.

## Greetings and sign-offs by recipient

| Recipient | Greeting | Sign-off |
|---|---|---|
| Teacher or professor | `Dear Professor [Surname],` / `Dear Dr. [Surname],` when known | `Best,` or `Best regards,` + full name |
| Admin or office staff | `Hi [First name],` if established; `Dear [Office] Team,` | `Best,`; keep an existing `Regards,` |
| Recruiter or employer | `Hi [First name],` / `Hi [Team] Team,`; `Dear [Title Surname],` if formal | `Best,` + full name |
| Senior professional or founder | `Hi [First name],` when established, else formal title | `Best,` or `Best regards,` |
| Peer or colleague | `Hi [First name],` | `Best,` + first name |
| Community or event organizer | `Hello [Org] team,` first time; `Hi [First name],` after | `Best,` or `Best regards,` |
| Vendor or support | `Hi [Team],` | `Best,` |

Never infer a title or honorific from a name alone. One sign-off and one signature; if the mail client adds a signature automatically, do not type another. Do not copy phone numbers from old emails; use only the one in the current thread's latest signature, and flag a mismatch.

## Language

- Request phrases (use one): `Could you please [action]?`, `Please let me know [specific question]`, `I wanted to ask if [request]`, `Would [option] work for you?`
- Offers: `I am happy to [action]` / `I'd be glad to [action]` only when the writer has offered it.
- Useful: `Thank you for [specific help].`, `Thank you so much for getting back to me.`, `That makes my next steps much clearer.`, `Looking forward to meeting you and the team.`, `I will keep you updated.`
- Full verb forms in formal mail; natural contractions in familiar or warm threads.
- Keep the thread subject when replying (replace any em dash with a colon or hyphen). New subjects: concrete, 4-10 words.

## Banned

- The em dash character (U+2014). Use periods, commas, colons, parentheses or a hyphen.
- "I hope this email finds you well" or any equivalent generic opener.
- "I wanted to reach out".
- "Please don't hesitate".
- "delve", "leverage" (as business filler).
- "I am truly passionate", "I genuinely believe", or any claim about the writer's own sincerity or passion.
- Elaborate compliments, inflated claims, rhetorical contrasts ("not just X, but Y").
- Flat filler acknowledgements with no warmth or next step ("I understand.", "Noted.").

## Fact discipline

State only supplied facts. Use `[PLACEHOLDER: what is missing]` for anything unknown (dates, names, job IDs, attachments). Never claim the writer attached, submitted, registered, joined, signed or completed anything unless the thread or request establishes it; write future intent instead ("I'll complete the form this week"). Never accept an offer, agree to terms or promise availability on the writer's behalf.

## Drafts only

Produce a draft for the user to review. Never send an email unless the user gives a separate, explicit instruction to send it.

## Self-check

- [ ] Purpose or answer in the first two sentences?
- [ ] Specific thanks or reaction, not generic warmth?
- [ ] Energetic and humble: one interest line max, at most one exclamation, learning/service framing?
- [ ] Concrete next step at the end?
- [ ] Length matches the incoming email; no padding?
- [ ] Every fact, date and commitment supported? Placeholders for anything missing?
- [ ] Zero em dashes, zero banned phrases?
- [ ] Exactly one sign-off and signature, correct contact details?

## Examples (all people and organizations are fictional)

**Loop-closing reply to a community organizer**

Hi Maya,

Thank you so much for getting back to me, and congratulations on filling every volunteer spot for the spring health summit. That says a lot about the community the Riverside Health Network has built!

I'll complete the interest form this week. As someone new to the local healthcare scene, I have a lot to learn from the network's members, and I'd be glad to pitch in wherever it helps most at the next event, whether that's setup, the registration desk, or session support.

Looking forward to meeting you and the team at an upcoming event.

Best,
[YOUR NAME]

**Loop-closing reply to a recruiter who sent guidance**

Hi Northwind Consulting Recruiting Team,

Thank you for such a thorough reply. Laying out the pathways by start date makes my next steps much clearer.

Since I finish my program in [MONTH YEAR], here is my plan:
- Keep my application for [role and job ID] active
- Join the Northwind talent mailing list and work through the recruiting guide
- Watch for the spring info sessions and take part in the case workshops

I'm excited by the chance to bring my background to Northwind's [practice area] work, and I look forward to meeting the team at an upcoming session.

Best,
[YOUR NAME]

Hi [First name],

Thank you for confirming. Thursday at 2 pm works for me, and I'll bring the updated numbers.

Best,

**Cold outreach**

Dear Dr. Okafor,

Your talk on reducing hospital readmissions at the Lakeside Care Conference changed how I think about discharge planning. I'm [CURRENT ROLE OR PROGRAM], working toward [GOAL].

Your point about follow-up calls within 48 hours stayed with me, and I have a lot to learn from how your team made it routine. Could you spare 15 minutes in the next few weeks for a call? I'd be glad to work around your schedule.

Best regards,
[YOUR NAME]

**Follow-up**

Hi Jordan,

Following up on my note from last Tuesday about the Q3 vendor list. Could you please send the final version by Friday so I can finish the review? Sorry for the short window, my timeline moved up.

Best,
[FIRST NAME]
